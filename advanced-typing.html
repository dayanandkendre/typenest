Master high-speed typing, advanced finger control, professional typing techniques, and achieve elite-level typing performance.

Introduction to Advanced Typing

Advanced typing represents the stage where a typist moves beyond basic speed and accuracy goals and begins focusing on professional-level performance. At this level, typing is no longer simply about finding keys quickly. Instead, it becomes a combination of speed, accuracy, rhythm, endurance, concentration, and consistency.

Many typists reach speeds of 40 to 60 words per minute and then struggle to improve further. This happens because advanced typing requires different techniques than beginner and intermediate typing. Small inefficiencies that were previously unnoticed become major obstacles when attempting to reach higher speeds.

This course is designed to help serious learners understand the habits, techniques, and training methods used by professional typists. By applying these principles consistently on TypeNest Practice Drills, learners can achieve significant improvements in both speed and accuracy.

Professional Typing Standards

Professional typists are expected to maintain both speed and accuracy. High speed alone is not enough if frequent errors reduce productivity. Most professional environments value consistent performance over occasional bursts of speed.

  • 60+ WPM – Strong Professional Level
  • 80+ WPM – Advanced Professional Level
  • 100+ WPM – Elite Typist Level
  • 95%+ Accuracy – Professional Standard
  • 98%+ Accuracy – Elite Standard

The goal of advanced training is to consistently perform at these levels during Timed Speed Tests under real-world conditions.

The Difference Between Fast and Advanced Typing

Many people assume that advanced typing simply means typing faster. However, advanced typing involves much more than raw speed. Professional typists maintain high speed while remaining accurate, relaxed, and efficient.

Fast typists often experience sudden speed fluctuations, fatigue, and inconsistent performance. Advanced typists maintain a steady pace regardless of paragraph length or difficulty.

The key difference lies in control. Advanced typists have developed strong muscle memory and refined finger movements that allow them to type efficiently for long periods.

Advanced Finger Control

At higher typing speeds, finger control becomes increasingly important. Even small unnecessary movements can reduce overall performance and create errors.

Advanced typists focus on minimizing finger travel distance and maintaining smooth transitions between keys. Every movement should feel natural and efficient.

  • Return fingers to home row consistently.
  • Avoid excessive hand movement.
  • Maintain relaxed wrists.
  • Use all fingers according to proper placement rules.
  • Develop precise finger coordination.

Improved finger control directly contributes to higher speed and better accuracy.

Eliminating Micro Delays

One of the biggest barriers to advanced typing is the presence of micro delays. These are tiny pauses that occur between letters, words, or sentences.

Although these delays may last only fractions of a second, they can significantly reduce overall typing speed when repeated hundreds of times throughout a document.

Common causes of micro delays include:

  • Hesitation before difficult words.
  • Weak muscle memory.
  • Looking at the keyboard.
  • Lack of confidence.
  • Inconsistent typing rhythm.

Eliminating these delays is essential for reaching professional-level typing speeds.

Building Typing Endurance

Advanced typists must maintain performance over long periods. Typing quickly for thirty seconds is very different from maintaining high performance for thirty minutes.

Endurance training helps improve stamina and reduces fatigue during extended typing sessions. The goal is to maintain speed and accuracy consistently from beginning to end.

Effective endurance training includes long paragraph practice, extended typing sessions, and gradual increases in training duration.

Advanced Accuracy Techniques

As typing speed increases, maintaining accuracy becomes more challenging. Advanced typists develop strategies that allow them to type quickly while minimizing mistakes.

  • Reading ahead while typing.
  • Maintaining visual focus on the screen.
  • Using consistent finger placement.
  • Developing strong muscle memory.
  • Practicing difficult word combinations.

Advanced accuracy training ensures that speed improvements do not come at the expense of quality.

The Importance of Rhythm & Mental Focus

Rhythm is one of the defining characteristics of advanced typists. Rather than typing in bursts, professional typists maintain a smooth and consistent flow. Mental focus plays a major role in performance—concentration helps typists maintain accuracy, recognize word patterns quickly, and reduce hesitation.

Advanced Speed Drills

Advanced speed drills are designed to push typing performance beyond ordinary practice levels. These exercises help develop finger coordination, rhythm, and rapid word recognition.

Reading Ahead Technique & Breaking Speed Plateaus

One of the most effective techniques used by advanced typists is reading ahead—processing upcoming words early so the brain prepares finger movements in advance.

When facing a speed plateau, challenge yourself with unfamiliar content, practice longer paragraphs, and increase test duration to continue building speed.

How to Reach 80 WPM and 100 WPM

Achieving 80 to 100 WPM places a typist among top performers. Reaching this milestone requires high-quality practice, maintaining 95%+ accuracy, minimizing hand movements, and building disciplined daily habits.

Professional Training Routine & Final Thoughts

Spend 5 minutes warming up, 10 minutes on speed drills, 10 minutes on long paragraph typing, and 5 minutes on error review. Advanced typing is the result of years of refinement, practice, and dedication. Stay patient, remain consistent, and continue practicing with purpose.
