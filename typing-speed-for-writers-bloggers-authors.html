How Fast Typing Helps Writers, Bloggers & Authors Finish Content 2x Faster

Writing is creative, but the physical keyboard is often the bottleneck. Learn how typing at 70+ WPM preserves cognitive flow, dissolves writer's block, and halves project drafting time.

📅 Updated: September 2026 • ⏱️ 6 min read • 👤 Dayanand Dinkar Kendre

📑 Table of Contents

Writing is often viewed as a purely creative process, but the mechanical barrier between your brain and the screen plays a massive role in your output. When you type at 30 WPM, your hands struggle to keep pace with your thoughts. By the time your fingers finish one sentence, the spontaneous brilliance of the next idea often evaporates.

Increasing your typing speed to 70+ WPM transforms your keyboard into an invisible conduit. It enables you to draft articles, book chapters, and blog posts at the speed of thought without losing creative momentum.

1. Preserving Cognitive Flow & Eliminating Friction

Psychologists define "flow state" as a mental condition where a creator is fully immersed in their task with high focus. Slow typing acts as a continuous series of micro-interruptions:

  • Looking down at keycaps breaks visual concentration on narrative structure.
  • Slow finger movement forces working memory to hold sentences longer, causing cognitive fatigue.
  • High WPM touch typing automates the mechanics so 100% of your mental bandwidth focuses on storytelling and word choice.

2. Overcoming Writer's Block with Fast Drafting

Writer's block is frequently caused by over-analyzing sentences while typing them. Professional authors advocate for the "Zero Draft" technique—writing down ideas as fast as possible without self-censoring or correcting minor typos.

💡 The Speed-Drafting Advantage:

  • When typing at 70 to 90 WPM, your internal critic cannot keep up to judge or stall your sentences.
  • You complete initial chapter outlines in a fraction of the time, providing real raw material to edit later.

3. The Math of Word Counts: Hours Saved per Project

Doubling your typing speed yields massive cumulative time savings across long-form writing projects:

Project Type Word Count Time at 35 WPM Time at 70 WPM Total Hours Saved
Detailed Blog Post 2,000 words ~57 mins ~28 mins 0.5 Hours
Non-Fiction Ebook 25,000 words ~11.9 hours ~5.9 hours 6.0 Hours
Full Length Novel 80,000 words ~38 hours ~19 hours 19.0 Hours

4. Separating Drafting Speed from Editing Precision

Fast typing does not mean sloppy final publications. High-speed authors divide their routine into two strict phases:

  1. Phase 1 (Rapid Drafting): Type continuously at max WPM to get thoughts on screen, deliberately ignoring minor typos.
  2. Phase 2 (Polishing & Editing): Slow down to review, correct grammar, rewrite weak sentences, and refine prose style.
