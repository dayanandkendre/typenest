01. The 15-Mark Statement Breakdown

In our overarching Government Typing Exam GCC-TBC Guide, we noted that the practical examination is partitioned into speed passage transcription, formal letter formatting, and spreadsheet statement preparation.

The 15 marks allocated to the statement question evaluate four primary competencies:

01 / Data Accuracy (6 Marks)

Error-Free Transcription

Entering item descriptions, serial numbers, accounting account heads, and monetary values without omitted or transposed digits.

02 / Alignment & Merges (4 Marks)

Cell Justification Rules

Applying left alignment to text labels, right alignment to numerical values, center alignment to serial numbers, and proper title merging across columns.

03 / Formulas & Functions (3 Marks)

Dynamic Totals

Using official spreadsheet formulas (such as SUM, AVERAGE, or PRODUCT) rather than typing calculated values by hand.

04 / Table Borders & Styling (2 Marks)

Visual Layout Compliance

Applying proper interior thin gridlines, bold outer borders, double bottom underlines for grand totals, and designated font sizes.

02. Alignment, Font & Border Rules

Standard typographical rules dictate how data is arranged across financial tables. Violating standard cell alignments results in automatic mark deductions:

Main Statement Heading Center (Merge & Center) 14pt – 16pt Font, Bold, Uppercase Merging beyond active columns
Serial Numbers (Sr. No.) Center Consistent column width (6–8 chars) Left-aligned numbers

03. Mandatory Excel Formulas

A major point of failure for GCC-TBC candidates is typing pre-calculated totals by reading them directly from the printed question sheet. In modern computerized testing platforms, the software checks cell formulas dynamically. If you enter 45000 directly into a total cell instead of inserting an active calculation, the system assigns zero marks for formula criteria.

Standard Functions Required in Statements

  • Column Sum: =SUM(D5:D14)
  • Row Total (Price * Quantity): =PRODUCT(C5, D5) or =C5*D5
  • Average Calculation: =AVERAGE(E5:E14)
  • Deduction (Net Salary = Gross - PF): =E5-F5

04. Common Traps & Deductions

During statement evaluation, minor oversights can accumulate penalties quickly:

  1. Truncated Text (### Errors): When a column is slightly narrower than the entered figure or date, Excel renders hash symbols (###). Always double-click column boundary dividers to auto-fit cell widths.
  2. Missing Column Headers: Forgetting to bold column titles (Ctrl + B) or neglecting to center-align headers above right-aligned numerical columns.
  3. Improper Decimal Formatting: Mixing raw integer figures with decimal values. If currency requires two decimal places, apply numeric formatting uniformly across the column.
  4. Incomplete Gridlines: Applying borders only to interior data cells while neglecting header rows or total summaries.

05. Step-by-Step Practical Routine

Execute your spreadsheet tasks using this disciplined four-phase approach during practical tests:

1

Phase 1: Raw Input (Minutes 1–6)

Type all labels, serial numbers, and numeric values continuously without pausing to format fonts, borders, or colors.

2

Phase 2: Mathematical Functions (Minutes 7–9)

Enter all necessary calculation formulas for column totals, subtotals, and deductions. Verify that calculated totals match question sheet values.

3

Phase 3: Alignment & Merges (Minutes 10–12)

Merge and center table titles across data columns. Right-align numeric columns, center-align serial numbers, and left-align descriptions.

4

Phase 4: Borders & Final Review (Minutes 13–15)

Apply all borders, double-check that no column truncates text into ###, and save your workbook file safely.

Conclusion

Scoring 15 out of 15 marks on the GCC-TBC Excel statement test is entirely achievable when you adhere to standardized typographical alignments and avoid hard-coded mathematical calculations. By combining disciplined numpad typing with structured cell formatting, you ensure reliable performance on test day.
